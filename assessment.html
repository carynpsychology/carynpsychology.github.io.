Assessment

Assessments are a structured way to gain a deeper understanding of specific concerns, whether for yourself or your child. Below, you'll find who this process is suited for, along with a clear overview of what to expect at each step.

Who is this for?

Children & Teens

Most assessments are conducted for children and adolescents when there are concerns about development or school functioning.

When is an assessment helpful?

  • Attention, focus, or hyperactivity (e.g. ADHD)
  • Social or communication differences (e.g. autism)
  • Learning or developmental delays
  • Ongoing challenges in school

What the assessment may include

  • Cognitive (IQ) testing
  • Behavioural and developmental measures (e.g. Conners, GARS, Vineland)
  • Clinical evaluation to understand your child's profile

The goal is to provide a clear understanding and guide appropriate support.

Adults

For adults, assessments may be helpful in understanding:

  • Attention-related concerns (e.g. ADHD)
  • Autism or long-standing social differences
  • Patterns that have affected functioning over time

You may not always require a formal assessment. In some cases, these concerns can also be explored through therapy.

What the process looks like

Initial Consultation (Recommended first)

  • Understand concerns and decide if assessment is needed.

Assessment Sessions (2 – 4 sessions)

Testing is usually done across multiple sessions. The number of sessions depend on the child's age, attention span, and individual needs.

  • Each session: 60–90 minutes
  • In-person only

Scoring & Interpretation

All results are carefully analysed and integrated.

Feedback Session

You'll receive:

  • Clear explanation of findings
  • Diagnosis (if applicable)
  • Practical recommendations
  • A concise written report for formal use
